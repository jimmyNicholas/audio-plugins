# Research: boundary-pushing ideas in music philosophy, aesthetics and practice (2016 to 2026)

Researched 2026-11-01 to feed thinking for future devices in this repo. Framing device: TAP, "The Annoying Piano" (`devices/tap/docs/SPEC.md`), a Max for Live MIDI effect for Ableton Live 11 (Max 8) where every key you release retunes keys you play later, so the instrument resists and collaborates with the player.

## How to read this

- **Method caveat:** the sandbox could not fetch pages directly. `www.hollyherndon.com` and `holly.plus` failed DNS resolution, and per the session's network rules no further direct fetches were attempted. Every claim below therefore comes from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, not a full read, and is marked **[extract]**. The researcher's own reasoning, links between ideas and design suggestions are marked **[inference]**. A small number of well-known facts the researcher brought in without a fetched source are marked **[background, unverified]**.
- **Source quality:** the brief asked for primary sources (the thinkers' own books, papers, talks, manifestos, statements and first-party interviews). Where the primary source exists, it is cited, but because pages could not be opened, what the source *says* often reached the researcher through a search summary of that page, or through a review of a book. Where only a review or press piece carried a claim, that is said in the line. Books are cited with publisher and year. Treat quotations as close paraphrase until checked against the original.
- **Confidence:** High = several independent sources agree and the primary source is identified. Medium = one or two sources, or the primary is identified but its wording is only known through a summary. Low = inference only, or the claim rests on a weak secondary source.
- **Scope:** "2016 to 2026" is about when the idea became prominent or was restated. Some roots are older (Oblique Strategies 1975, Oliveros from the 1980s, Fisher's hauntology from the mid 2000s); they are included where the 2016 to 2026 period gave them new force.

The strongest single thread for TAP **[inference]**: three separate bodies of work (NIME instrument research, Magnusson's philosophy of instruments, and Herndon's AI practice) converge on the same move. They stop treating the instrument as a transparent tool that should obey, and start treating it as a partner with its own tendencies, whose resistance is where the music comes from.

## 1. AI as collaborator, and art made at the level of the protocol

### 1.1 PROTO and Spawn (2019)

- Holly Herndon's 2019 album *PROTO* (4AD) featured Spawn, an AI she framed as an ensemble member rather than a voice-cloning instrument. In MusicTech she says "We're trying to view Spawn as an ensemble member", wanting material performed by human and AI performers. **[extract]** https://www.musictech.com/features/interviews/holly-herndon-proto/
- The stated goal was not a virtual instrument that replicates Herndon's voice from MIDI, but an AI that is a musical collaborator. Spawn was developed with Mat Dryhurst and programmer Jules LaPlace and trained on the voices of Herndon and her ensemble. Because Spawn did not run in real time, writing sessions were asynchronous. **[extract]** same MusicTech interview; https://www.spinmagazine.com/2019/06/ai-music-artificial-intelligence-feature-holly-herndon-yacht/ (Spin describes Spawn as a member of the ensemble "rather than its omniscient director").
- *PROTO* was part of her Stanford CCRMA doctoral work. In Tape Op she says the dissertation "consisted of a body of work which is PROTO, and [the performance pieces] 'Deep Belief' and 'Chain Opera,' and then an analysis of that". **[extract]** https://tapeop.com/interviews/132/holly-herndon ; https://stereogum.com/2041686/holly-herndon-proto-interview/interviews
- Training data for much of the album came from audio recorded during a public performance at Berlin's ISM Hexadome, so the audience became part of the dataset. **[extract]** Spin, above.

### 1.2 Holly+ and shared ownership of a voice (2021 to 2022)

- Holly+ (2021, built with Never Before Heard Sounds) is a browser tool that re-sings uploaded audio in Herndon's voice. She set up a DAO to govern her digital likeness, saying "This development raises novel questions about voice ownership that I think can be addressed by DAO governance." **[extract]** https://decrypt.co/75958/holly-herndon-launches-dao-controlled-vocal-deepfake-platform-holly ; https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/holly-herndon-ai-deepfake-tool-1197200/
- Reported split for approved uses: 50% to the work's creator, 40% to DAO members, 10% to Herndon. **[extract]** Decrypt, above. Sources differ on how many people hold DAO seats; Water & Music's deep dive explains why the DAO moved slowly. **[extract]** https://www.waterandmusic.com/music-dao-deep-dives-pt-10-holly-plus
- TED2022 talk, "What if you could sing in your favorite musician's voice?", performed live with the singer Pher alongside Holly+. Herndon and co-founders (Dryhurst, Jordan Meyer, Patrick Hoepner) use "spawning" for generating new media from a training set. **[extract]** https://www.ted.com/speakers/holly_herndon ; https://newsuns.net/holly-herndon-spawning-identities/ ; https://documentjournal.com/2022/01/who-does-your-voice-belong-to-for-musician-holly-herndon-the-answer-is-everyone

### 1.3 Spawning, consent tools and "protocol art"

- Spawning (co-founded 2022) built Have I Been Trained (search whether your work is in the LAION-5B image dataset) and a Do Not Train registry. Opting out only affects future training. **[extract]** https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/learn/tcai-guide-to-search-tools-was-your-data-used-to-train-an-ai-model ; https://kompozy.io/how-to/opt-out-of-ai-training-on-your-content (both secondary; Spawning's own site was not reached).
- In a 2025 Resident Advisor interview the pair said: "We're pro-AI and we're pro-consent. Those things don't have to be mutually exclusive." **[extract]** https://ra.co/exchange/789 (quote surfaced via search summary of the RA Exchange and related coverage).
- **Protocol art:** their term for treating the systems, protocols and rules that shape cultural production as the artistic medium; the dataset itself is part of the artwork. Herndon places the creative act "upstream" in setting the rule set and conditions in which art is made. **[extract]** https://art.stanford.edu/events/holly-herndon-mat-dryhurst-protocol-art ; https://engadin-art-talks.ch/en/videos/protocol-art ; https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/experimental-composer-holly-herndon-built-an-ai-voice-clone-that-anyone-can/ . No standalone essay titled "Protocol Art" was found; the closest written statement is their book *All Media is Training Data* (König Books, 2024), which was not verified in this session. **[extract, Medium]**
- *The Call* (Serpentine, London, 2024): they composed a songbook and a recording protocol, recorded fifteen community choirs across the UK, and trained a model on the result. The choirs were part of a data trust experiment, written up in a 2025 white paper. **[extract]** https://www.serpentinegalleries.org/whats-on/holly-herndon-mat-dryhurst-the-call/ ; https://zenodo.org/records/14859320 ; https://ocula.com/magazine/features/holly-herndon-and-mat-dryhurst-serpentine-call/
- **Data dignity:** the brief linked this term to Herndon and Dryhurst. Searches found it attached to them only on a low-authority student blog; the term is usually credited to Jaron Lanier **[background, unverified]**. Herndon and Dryhurst's own vocabulary is consent, data trusts and protocol. **Dropped as a Herndon/Dryhurst claim. [Low]**

**Takeaway [inference]:** Herndon's lasting move is not "AI makes music" but "the rules and data are the composition". For a Max device, that means the interesting design object is the *rule set* the device enforces (as TAP's modes already are), and who gets to set it.

## 2. Generative AI at scale, and the consent fight

- **Google Magenta:** DDSP (Differentiable Digital Signal Processing) was open-sourced in 2020; Tone Transfer (October 2020) re-renders a sung or played line as another instrument in the browser; DDSP-VST (2022) runs it as a real-time plug-in in a DAW. **[extract]** https://magenta.tensorflow.org/tone-transfer ; https://magenta.tensorflow.org/ddsp-vst-blog ; https://blog.google/technology/ai/what-if-you-could-turn-your-voice-any-instrument/ ; https://research.google/pubs/pub50554
- **Suno and Udio:** the RIAA sued both in June 2024 on behalf of the major labels for training on copyrighted recordings. Universal settled with Udio in late October 2025 with licensing and a move to a licensed platform in 2026; Udio disabled downloads. Warner settled with Udio and then with Suno (November 2025), Suno agreeing to replace its models with licensed ones in 2026 and to restrict downloads. As of April 2026, Universal and Sony versus Suno were reported at an impasse. **[extract]** https://gulfnews.com/business/markets/universal-music-settles-lawsuit-with-ai-startup-udio-1.500326712 ; https://decrypt.co/349360/warner-music-moves-from-litigation-to-licensing-with-udio ; https://news.bgov.com/ip-law/warner-music-settles-lawsuit-with-ai-startup-suno ; https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/04/09/suno-universal-music-lawsuit-settlement-impasse/ . Status after April 2026 was not checked. **[Medium for current status]**

**Takeaway [inference]:** the industrial AI story ends in "walled gardens" and licensing, which is the opposite of the ensemble model in section 1. Small, local, rule-based devices (like TAP) sit outside this fight: no training data, no consent problem, and the "intelligence" is legible.

## 3. Decolonising tuning: 12-TET as a default, not a law

- Khyam Allami, with the studio Counterpoint, released two free browser tools in 2021: **Leimma** for exploring and making tuning systems, and **Apotome** for generative composition with them. They respond to the bias that modern production tools inherit from Western theory. **[extract]** https://cdm.link/escaping-tunings-restrictive-default-setting-a-conversation-with-khyam-allami/ ; https://cdm.link/sonic-worlds-liberated-listen-to-performances-on-the-free-tuning-tool-apotome/
- Allami's own line, used as the headline of a September 2021 conversation with Anders Reuter: "Equal temperament is the McDonald's of tuning." **[extract]** https://passiveaggressive.dk/?p=31106 ; also Ableton's blog, https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/latent-sonorites-a-transcultural-musical-proposal/ . He is also quoted: "we've come to believe that equal temperament is embedded within Western instruments and Western music, but it really never was." **[extract]** https://www.researchcatalogue.net/view/1656067/1661190
- His CTM Festival 2021 workshop, "Repressed Possibilities: Tuning, Technology and Western bias in non-Western Music Making", framed tools as imposing "the hegemony of twelve-tone equal-temperament on pitch" (festival copy, not necessarily his words). **[extract]** https://www.ctm-festival.de/festival-2021/programme/practice/a2a-transmission/workshop-1-khyam-allami
- **Ableton followed:** Live 12 (2024) supports tuning systems beyond 12-TET, reading Scala `.scl` and Ableton's `.ascl` (which adds a reference pitch), with a companion site at tuning.ableton.com. The free Max for Live **Microtuner** pack (2022) loads scale files and morphs between tunings with MPE. **[extract]** https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/using-tuning-systems/ ; https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/11535414344476 ; https://www.ableton.com/en/packs/microtuner/ ; https://cdm.link/2022/03/ableton-releases-free-microtuner-but-tuning-is-more-than-spaces-between-12-semitones/ . CDM's 2022 headline is itself a critique: "tuning is more than spaces between 12 semitones".
- **ODDSound MTS-ESP** (with Aphex Twin): a master tuning source for a DAW, building on the 1992 MIDI Tuning Standard, that can also retune non-aware instruments via MPE or pitch bend; core library BSD-licensed. **[extract]** https://cdm.link/inside-mts-esp-the-new-tuning-tool-from-oddsound-and-aphex-twin/ ; https://www.synthtopia.com/?p=122887

**Takeaway [inference]:** TAP already treats 12-TET as merely the starting state that playing erodes. The critique above suggests going further: let the *starting* state be any scale, and let tuning drift be culturally informed (for example, drifting towards maqam intervals or just ratios, as TAP's Harmony mode does) rather than random.

## 4. Microtonality goes mainstream in rock, pop and jazz

- **King Gizzard and the Lizard Wizard**, *Flying Microtonal Banana* (February 2017), subtitled "Explorations into Microtonal Tuning, Volume 1", uses 24-tone equal temperament. Stu Mackenzie's guitar was refretted by luthier Zac Eccles after he met the Turkish bağlama on tour. Mackenzie described the aim as adding "secret notes, sort of out of tune, like out of tune half, quarter-tones". **[extract]** https://www.premierguitar.com/artists/microtonal-madmen-king-gizzard-and-the-lizard-wizards-stu-mackenzie-and-joey-walker ; https://guitar.com/news/music-news/king-gizzard-stu-mackenzie-discusses-microtonal/ . The band later returned to it, calling it "one of the funnest experiences we ever had". **[extract]** same sources. (*K.G.* in 2020 was its sequel **[background, unverified]**; see the NME interview https://www.nme.com/features/king-gizzard-and-the-lizard-wizard-album-kg-interview-2020-2833101.)
- **Jacob Collier**, *In the Bleak Midwinter* (2016): between verses three and four he modulates from E to a key a quarter tone sharp ("G half-sharp"), shifting the whole tuning frame while singing. **[extract]** https://ethanhein.com/wp/2019/jacob-colliers-four-magical-chords/ (secondary analysis; Collier's own video explanation is linked from it). An Edith Cowan University honours thesis calls this "contextual tuning". **[extract]** https://ro.ecu.edu.au/theses_hons/1562 . Collier on his motive: microtonality is "almost like a deliberate effort to break my perfect pitch open". **[extract]** https://www.musictech.net/news/music/jacob-collier-microtonality-deliberate-effort-break-perfect-pitch-open/

**Takeaway [inference]:** Collier's case is directly TAP-like: the reference pitch itself moves through a performance, and the ear follows. That legitimises drift as a musical, not merely annoying, effect.

## 5. Listening ethics: Dylan Robinson's *Hungry Listening*

- Dylan Robinson, *Hungry Listening: Resonant Theory for Indigenous Sound Studies* (University of Minnesota Press, 2020, Indigenous Americas series). Robinson is Stó:lō. **[extract]** https://utpdistribution.com/9781517907693/hungry-listening
- "Hungry listening" translates two Halq'eméylem words (roughly the settler as "starving person", plus listening) and names a settler colonial perceptual logic that consumes sound without reciprocity. Robinson treats listening as an orientation rather than a fixed behaviour. **[extract]** reviews: https://iaspmjournal.net/index.php/IASPM_Journal/article/download/1103/pdf/7537 ; https://feralfeminisms.com/listening-for-worlds-without-me-a-book-review-of-hungry-listening-resonant-theory-for-indigenous-studies/
- "Inclusionary" performance, in his critical sense, fits Indigenous people and content into Western composition and performance structures; he proposes "Indigenous+art music" to keep the two sovereign and often incompatible, and "apposite methodologies" that write *with* rather than *about*. **[extract]** reviews in IASPM Journal (above) and MUSICultures, https://journals.lib.unb.ca/index.php/MC/article/download/32789/1882528001/1882533681
- The claim that Robinson frames some Indigenous song as law rather than art was not confirmed in the extracts. **Dropped. [Low]**

**Takeaway [inference]:** for device design, Robinson is a caution, not a feature list. A "world tunings" menu that offers maqam or gamelan as flavours risks exactly the extractive, inclusionary pattern he describes. Allami's approach (tools that let people build their own systems) is the more respectful model.

## 6. The instrument has agency, and contains theory

### 6.1 Thor Magnusson, *Sonic Writing* (2019)

- Thor Magnusson, *Sonic Writing: Technologies of Material, Symbolic, and Signal Inscriptions* (Bloomsbury Academic, 2019). Part I includes a chapter titled "Epistemic Tools". **[extract]** https://monoskop.org/log/?p=21494 ; https://sussex.figshare.com/articles/book/Sonic_writing_technologies_of_material_symbolic_and_signal_inscriptions/23463899
- Per a review in the Journal for Artistic Research: the instrument is not a neutral medium between idea and work but an "epistemic tool" that embodies music theory; instruments are "containers of theory" that reveal some things and conceal others; for digital instruments, designing the instrument "becomes indistinguishable from the designing of a new music theory". **[extract]** https://jar-online.net/en/node/1994 (review, Medium).

### 6.2 Actuated and "feral" instruments

- Tom Davis, "The Feral Cello: A Philosophically Informed Approach to an Actuated Instrument", NIME 2017. Sets itself against Machover's hyperinstruments, which aimed at "absolute control", learnability and repeatability; instead, drawing on post-phenomenology, treats human and instrument as co-defining and recognises the instrument's agency in improvisation. **[extract]** https://nime.org/proc/nime2017_tdavis/
- Davis and Laura Reid, "Taking Back Control: Taming the Feral Cello", NIME 2020: after about four years of use, the system's unpredictability is meant to stop the performer settling into a consistent mode, making it "a disruptive technological partner". **[extract]** https://nime.org/proc/nime2020_81/
- Robert Ek, "Playing with resistance", NIME 2024: one could argue a skilled performer aims to remove resistance, but resistance is "a central part of the relationship between performer and instrument" and a design resource. **[extract]** https://nime.org/proc/nime2024_24/
- Tom Mudd, "Playing with Feedback: Unpredictability, Immediacy, and Entangled Agency in the No-input Mixing Desk", CHI 2023, and Mudd and Brown, "Musical pathways through the no-input mixer", NIME 2023 (22 artist interviews). **[extract]** https://nime.org/proc/nime2023_56/ ; abstract of the CHI paper not reached. Companion: Morrison and McPherson, "Entangling Entanglement", CHI 2024. **[extract, Medium]**

### 6.3 Andrew McPherson's group: constraint, values and longevity

- Zappi and McPherson, "Dimensionality and Appropriation in Digital Musical Instrument Design", NIME 2014: ten musicians given a one-sensor cube; adding a second degree of freedom *reduced* exploration. **[extract]** https://nime.org/proc/nime2014_vzappi/ (pre-2016, but the base of later work).
- Zappi and McPherson, "Hackable Instruments: Supporting Appropriation and Modification in Digital Musical Interaction", Frontiers in ICT, 2018: design choices encourage or discourage appropriation; a highly restrictive instrument provoked unexpected creative use. **[extract]** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ict/articles/10.3389/fict.2018.00026/text
- Morreale, McPherson and Wanderley, "NIME Identity from the Performer's Perspective", NIME 2018 (78 performers): new instruments are exploratory, rarely finished, and learning rarely leads to virtuosity in the traditional sense. Morreale and McPherson, "Design for Longevity", NIME 2017: most NIME instruments from 2010 to 2014 did not establish themselves. **[extract]** https://nime.org/proc/morreale2018 ; https://nime.org/proc/fmorreale2017
- Lepri and McPherson, "Making Up Instruments: Design Fiction for Value Discovery in Communities of Musical Practice" (DIS 2019) and "Fictional Instruments, Real Values" (NIME 2019); "Embrace the Weirdness: Negotiating Values Inscribed into Music Technology" (Computer Music Journal, 2021). **[extract]** https://dblp.org/pid/131/3650 ; https://qmro.qmul.ac.uk/xmlui/handle/123456789/71109
- The brief's "musical instruments as scripts" paper was not found under that title. **Dropped. [Low]**

**Takeaway [inference]:** this is TAP's intellectual home. TAP is a feral piano: resistance as design resource (Ek), a deliberately constrained control surface with one Mod knob (Zappi and McPherson), and a mode list that is literally a small music theory (Magnusson). The longevity findings warn that novelty alone fades; devices need depth a player can grow into.

## 7. Live coding: show your screens, and the code is the instrument

- The TOPLAP draft manifesto (begun 2004 on a bus to Lübeck airport) demands "Give us access to the performer's mind, to the whole human instrument" and states "Obscurantism is dangerous. Show us your screens." **[extract]** https://tidalcycles.org/docs/around_tidal/toplap_manifesto ; https://blog.toplap.org/wiki/ManifestoDraft . A TOPLAP forum thread (2020s) proposes rewriting it from scratch. **[extract]** https://forum.toplap.org/t/toplap-manifesto-draft-review-and-update/1995
- Alan Blackwell, Emma Cocker, Geoff Cox, Alex McLean and Thor Magnusson, *Live Coding: A User's Manual* (MIT Press, 2022, Software Studies series, open access, CC BY-SA). **[extract]** https://livecodingbook.toplap.org/ ; https://directory.doabooks.org/handle/20.500.12854/96972
- **Strudel** (2022): a JavaScript port of TidalCycles by Felix Roos with Alex McLean (yaxu), running entirely in the browser to make live coding more accessible, with visual feedback and output to Web Audio, OSC and Web MIDI. Paper: Roos and McLean, "Strudel: Live Coding Patterns on the Web", ICLC 2023. **[extract]** https://zenodo.org/records/6821023 ; https://cdm.link/2022/04/musical-powerful-live-coding-in-the-browser-is-near-with-strudel-usable-now/
- McLean is a founder of the algorave movement; his 2025 post "algoraves are here right now" is a first-party statement of its current state. **[extract]** https://slab.org/2025/03/18/algoraves-are-here-right-now/

**Takeaway [inference]:** "show us your screens" is a transparency ethic. An "annoying" device is fairer and more playable if it shows its rule working (what moved, why, by how much), turning frustration into legible counterpoint.

## 8. Deep listening and acoustic ecology after 2016

- Pauline Oliveros died in November 2016 **[background, unverified]**; RPI published a remembrance that month. **[extract]** https://news.rpi.edu/content/2016/11/30/remembering-deep-listener-pioneer-pauline-oliveros
- Her definition, quoted by the Center for Deep Listening at RPI: Deep Listening is "a way of listening in every possible way to everything possible, to hear no matter what you are doing". The Center (successor to the Deep Listening Institute, at RPI from 2013/2014) released *A Year of Deep Listening: 365 Text Scores for Pauline Oliveros* in 2025, and a 2026 Venice Biennale presentation featured her work. **[extract]** https://www.deeplistening.rpi.edu/about-us/ ; https://news.rpi.edu/2025/05/13/celebration-sound-and-silence-rpi-releases-book-honoring-pauline-oliveros ; https://news.rpi.edu/2026/04/30/venice-biennale-feature-late-rpi-professor-pauline-oliveros
- Pauline Oliveros, *Quantum Listening* (Ignota Books, 2022, foreword by Laurie Anderson, introduction by IONE): a short manifesto presenting Deep Listening as the basis of a transformed social order. The original text dates from 1999 or 2010 depending on source. **[extract]** https://thequietus.com/?p=31805 ; https://www.stroom.nl/en/stroom/library/quantum-listening
- Bernie Krause divides environmental sound into geophony (wind, water), biophony (living creatures) and anthrophony (humans and machines), and argues an ecosystem's health can be heard; he says half of the wild soundscapes in his archive no longer exist. **[extract]** https://www.nature.com/articles/485308a ; Krause, *The Great Animal Orchestra* (Little, Brown / Profile Books, 2012).
- *The Great Animal Orchestra* installation (Bernie Krause and United Visual Artists), commissioned by the Fondation Cartier, Paris, July 2016 to January 2017, built from his 5,000+ hours of recordings, then toured (Milan and London 2019, Biennale of Sydney, Peabody Essex Museum 2021 to 2022). **[extract]** https://www.fondationcartier.com/en/programme/exhibition/the-great-animal-orchestra ; https://www.uva.co.uk/features/great-animal-orchestra-cartier-foundation ; https://www.pem.org/press-news/pem-fondation-cartier-pour-lart-contemporain-present-the-great-animal-orchestra

**Takeaway [inference]:** Krause's niche idea (each species occupies its own frequency and time band) is a ready-made rule for a device that keeps voices out of each other's way, the cooperative opposite of TAP's Gravity mode.

## 9. Hyperpop, synthetic materiality and genre collapse

- A. G. Cook founded PC Music in August 2013; its exaggerated pop tropes became a foundation for "hyperpop", a word Spotify used as a playlist name in August 2019. A Spotify editor called Cook the "godfather" of hyperpop. PC Music stopped releasing new music in 2023. **[extract]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._G._Cook (tertiary; Cook's own interviews, for example https://www.rollingstone.co.uk/music/ag-cook-britpop-pc-music-interview-39355/ , were found but their text on this point was not reached). **[Medium]**
- SOPHIE (died January 2021 **[background, unverified]**): in a 2014 Billboard interview, all sounds except vocals were built from raw waveforms rather than samples, with attention to how a material's physical properties shape its sound, imagining materials that do not exist. *Oil of Every Pearl's Un-Insides* (2018) carried this forward; Artforum describes its synthesis as "synesthetically real". **[extract]** https://www.artforum.com/columns/sophie-1986-2021-249532/ ; https://www.thefader.com/2018/06/06/sophie-debut-album-oil-of-every-pearls-un-insides . The Billboard interview itself was not located by URL. **[Medium]**
- "Collapse of genre" as a thesis: no single primary source was found that owns it. The evidence is indirect: genre names minted by streaming playlists (hyperpop above) and the microgenre explosion. **[inference, Low]**

**Takeaway [inference]:** SOPHIE's method (physical behaviour of impossible materials) maps neatly onto MIDI too: a device could give pitch "material" properties such as elasticity, stickiness or brittleness, which is what TAP's Gravity and Repel modes already hint at.

## 10. Attention economy, mood playlists and algorithmic flattening

- Liz Pelly, *Mood Machine: The Rise of Spotify and the Costs of the Perfect Playlist* (One Signal / Atria, Simon & Schuster, January 2025). An excerpt in Harper's, "The Ghosts in the Machine", documented Spotify's "Perfect Fit Content" programme: commissioned, low-royalty tracks engineered to blend into mood playlists. **[extract]** https://www.simonandschuster.ca/books/Mood-Machine/Liz-Pelly/9781668083512 ; https://www.aristake.com/liz-pelly/ ; https://cdm.link/mood-machine-liz-pelly/ . Spotify denied producing the tracks. **[extract]**
- Pelly heard "lean-back listening" from a label digital marketer while reporting her first Spotify piece in 2016; her book argues streaming did not invent the lean-back listener but championed it to sell users to advertisers. Earlier Baffler essays: "The Problem with Muzak" (2017) and "Streambait Pop" (2018). **[extract]** https://recordstore.substack.com/p/interview-liz-pelly ; https://www.mackhagood.com/podcast/liz-pelly-on-spotify/
- Kyle Chayka, *Filterworld: How Algorithms Flattened Culture* (Doubleday, 2024): recommendation feeds replace human tastemakers and push creators, including musicians, to shape work around what performs in feeds. **[extract]** https://www.npr.org/transcripts/1224955473
- "Ambient as resistance": no primary source owning this phrase was found. Pelly's critique points the other way: ambient and chill have been the genres most absorbed into functional, background playlists. **[inference, Low]**

**Takeaway [inference]:** an instrument that refuses to be background (it changes when you stop paying attention, or punishes autopilot playing) is a small, concrete answer to lean-back design.

## 11. Hauntology after Mark Fisher

- Mark Fisher, *Ghosts of My Life: Writings on Depression, Hauntology and Lost Futures* (Zero Books, 2014): opens on "the slow cancellation of the future", a phrase he credits to Franco Berardi, and reads Derrida's hauntology through pop culture. Fisher died in January 2017. **[extract]** https://www.resilience.org/stories/2022-09-28/how-to-escape-the-slow-cancellation-of-the-future/ ; https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/72869133
- *k-punk: The Collected and Unpublished Writings of Mark Fisher (2004 to 2016)*, ed. Darren Ambrose (Repeater, 2018), includes the unfinished introduction to his planned *Acid Communism*. **[extract]** https://lrb.co.uk/v41/n09/jenny-turner/not-no-longer-but-not-yet
- After 2017 the ideas are mostly restated by others rather than extended by new primary work, so this thread is weaker than the rest for 2016 to 2026. **[inference, Medium]**

**Takeaway [inference]:** hauntology's sonic markers (degraded memory, tape decay, crackle) already underpin the slush-wave device in this repo. For a MIDI device, an analogue is *memory that decays*: a pitch table that slowly forgets the player's edits.

## 12. Disabled music-making and accessible instruments

- Open Up Music's **Clarion** (first launched in schools August 2016) turns an iPad or Windows device into an instrument playable with any part of the body, including eye movement; it won One-Handed Musical Instrument (OHMI) awards in 2015 and 2017 and a 2020 Tech4Good award; ABRSM added it to an assessment scheme in 2024; on general sale in the UK from 2025. **[extract]** https://openupmusic.org/clarion ; https://www.musicmark.org.uk/news/the-clarion-an-award-winning-accessible-instrument/
- Open Up Music founded the **National Open Youth Orchestra** (2018), described as the world's first disabled-led national youth orchestra. **[extract]** same Open Up Music sources.
- **Drake Music** DMLab builds accessible instruments (a 3D-printed light-to-sound device, a DJ set triggered by nose, chin or tongue) and runs hack days. **[extract]** https://www.drakemusic.org/technology/
- A Queen's University Belfast doctoral study with Drake Music Northern Ireland found accessible devices seldom see sustained use outside academia; inclusion depends on the wider ecosystem of facilitators and companion technologies. **[extract]** https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/studentTheses/exploring-and-cultivating-connections-within-ecosystems-of-inclus/
- **Ableton Live 12** (2024) is the first version of Live to support screen readers (VoiceOver, NVDA, Narrator, JAWS), with reworked keyboard navigation. Max for Live is listed as not yet supported for screen readers. **[extract]** https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/11550373507868 ; https://www.ableton.com/en/live-manual/12/accessibility-and-keyboard-navigation/

**Takeaway [inference]:** two direct implications. First, Max for Live devices are currently a blind spot for screen reader users, so any device here should have meaningful parameter names and info text and be fully usable from automation and MIDI mapping. Second, a retuning device lets a player with limited reach get rich pitch material from a few keys; TAP's Rotate and Move modes already do this almost by accident.

## 14. Design prompts for Max for Live devices like TAP

All prompts are **[inference]**. Each names the idea it comes from. Most can be built as Max 8 MIDI effects using the same pattern as TAP (ES5 `js` engine, MPE pitch bend out, `live.*` controls).

| # | Prompt | From | Fits TAP's architecture? |
|---|--------|------|--------------------------|
| P1 | **Show the rule.** A small display that names what just moved and by how many cents after each release, so the resistance is legible counterpoint, not a bug. | Live coding "show us your screens" (7); Ek (6.2) | Yes: the engine already knows. |
| P2 | **Any starting tuning.** Load a Scala `.scl` / `.ascl` file as the "concert pitch" TAP drifts from; Reset returns to that scale, not 12-TET. | Allami (3); Live 12 tuning (3) | Yes: replace the initial pitch table. |
| P3 | **Cultural attractors, built by the player.** Gravity pulls towards intervals in a user-made scale (made in Leimma or Live 12) rather than towards the last key. Avoid a "world tunings" preset menu. | Allami (3); Robinson (5) | Yes: new mode. |
| P4 | **Moving reference.** A mode where the whole keyboard's reference slides by a fraction of a tone over a phrase, Collier style, so modulation goes off the 12-grid. | Collier (4) | Yes: global offset. |
| P5 | **Material pitch.** Modes that give each key a physical property: elastic (springs back to home over time), sticky (resists change, then jumps), brittle (snaps to a random far pitch after too many edits). | SOPHIE (9); feral cello (6.2) | Yes: per-key state plus a timer task. |
| P6 | **Forgetting.** The pitch table decays back towards its start at a set rate, so the instrument remembers you only as long as you keep playing. | Fisher / hauntology (11); Oliveros (8) | Yes: `task` in `js`. |
| P7 | **Attention penalty.** If the player repeats the same pattern (autopilot), drift increases; varied playing calms it. The anti-lean-back instrument. | Pelly, Chayka (10) | Yes: pattern memory in engine. |
| P8 | **Niche keeper.** A cooperative mode: notes that would clash with currently held notes are nudged into a free pitch band (Krause's acoustic niche), the friendly twin of Repel. | Krause (8) | Yes. |
| P9 | **Two-player ensemble.** Two TAP instances on two tracks share one pitch table via `send`/`receive`, so each player retunes the other's keys. The instrument becomes the ensemble. | Herndon's ensemble framing (1.1) | Mostly: shared state across devices needs care in Live. |
| P10 | **Rules as the composition.** Let players save and share mode rule sets (small JSON "protocols"), so the shareable artefact is the rule, not the notes. | Protocol art (1.3); Magnusson (6.1) | Yes: preset file of parameters plus mode definitions. |
| P11 | **One degree of freedom.** Keep a single expressive control (TAP's Mod knob) and resist adding more; test with players whether each new knob reduces exploration. | Zappi and McPherson (6.3) | Design policy, not a feature. |
| P12 | **Accessible by default.** Every control named, with info text, mappable and automatable, nothing that requires the mouse; a "few keys, big world" preset (Rotate or Move with wide ranges) for players with limited reach. | Accessibility (12) | Yes. |
| P13 | **Oblique mode change.** A button that applies a random mode and Mod value, logged on screen, honouring the error as intention. | Oblique Strategies (13) | Yes. |
| P14 | **Local intelligence, no data.** If any learning is added (for example, a Markov model of the player's last 64 notes steering drift), keep it local, inspectable and resettable, with nothing trained on other people's music. | Consent fight (1.3, 2) | Yes, within ES5 limits. |

Open questions for the team:

- Does legible resistance (P1) make TAP more playable, or does hiding the rule keep the "annoying" character? Worth a quick player test.
- Shared state between two Max for Live devices (P9) needs checking against how Live 11 handles global `send`/`receive` across tracks and saved sets.
- Live 12's native tuning systems may make P2 partly redundant for Live 12 users, but this repo targets Live 11 / Max 8, so a device-side scale loader still matters.
