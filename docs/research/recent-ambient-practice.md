# Research: recent practice in ambient, dark ambient and slushwave (2016 to 2026)

Researched 2026-11-01 to feed device ideas for this repo (Max for Live, Ableton Live 11, Max 8). Focus: pitched-down and slowed techniques, and generative or process-based methods that use no AI or machine learning.

## How to read this

- **Method caveat:** direct page fetches mostly failed in this sandbox (`temporaryresidence.com`, `lightintheattic.net` and `www.macworld.com` all failed DNS resolution, and per the session's network rules no further sites were tried). Three primary sources were read in full: the Paulstretch home page on SourceForge, and the PaulXStretch README and `Stretch.cpp` source file (cloned from GitHub). Those claims are marked **[read]**. Every other claim comes from search-engine extracts of the cited pages and is marked **[extract]**. The researcher's own reasoning and design suggestions are marked **[inference]**. A few facts brought in from general knowledge without a fetched source are marked **[background, unverified]**.
- **Source quality:** the brief asked for primary sources (artists' statements, liner notes, first-party interviews, label pages, tool docs and source code, academic papers). Where one was identified it is cited first. But because pages could not be opened, what a label page or interview *says* usually reached the researcher through a search summary. Treat quotations as close paraphrase until checked. Where only press or a tertiary source (Wikipedia, Know Your Meme) carried a claim, the line says so.
- **Confidence:** High = several independent sources agree and a primary source is identified. Medium = one or two sources, or a primary source known only through a summary. Low = inference, or a weak or conflicting source.
- **Scope:** 2016 to 2026, weighted to 2021 onwards. Older roots (Eno's 1970s loops, Paulstretch 2006, DJ Screw) appear only as background. This file is about *practice and practitioners*. It deliberately does not repeat:
  - the slushwave signal chain and its settings: see [slushwave-sound.md](../../devices/slush-wave/docs/research/slushwave-sound.md);
  - deep listening, acoustic ecology (Oliveros, Krause), hauntology (Fisher), and Liz Pelly's *Mood Machine* and Kyle Chayka's *Filterworld* in general: see [boundary-pushing-music-philosophy.md](boundary-pushing-music-philosophy.md) sections 8, 10 and 11;
  - tuning tools (Leimma, MTS-ESP, Live 12 tuning) and generative MIDI tools (Marbles, Turing Machine, Inspired by Nature): see [boundary-pushing-plugins.md](boundary-pushing-plugins.md) sections 1 and 2.
- **AI-free:** every technique in the TL;DR is marked. "Yes" means the method as described by its practitioners uses no machine learning: it is tape, DSP, rules, chance or physical process. Where an adjacent product is AI-driven, the section says so.

The strongest single thread **[inference]:** across ambient, drone and slowed-music scenes the recent energy is not in new effects but in *process with consequences*: material that wears out (Basinski, Hainbach, Amulets), tunings that make chords beat (Malone, Arkbro, Davachi), rules that change with time (Reflection, ASLSP), and slowing that changes the meaning of a pop song (slowed + reverb). All of it is AI-free, and nearly all of it can be modelled in Max 8.

## 1. Tape decay and archival loops

### 1.1 William Basinski after *The Disintegration Loops*

- Background to the legacy: the *Disintegration Loops* tapes, made in 1982, flaked off while Basinski transferred them to digital, so preserving them destroyed them. **[extract]** https://textura.org/reviews/basinksi_disintegrationloops.htm ; Basinski in his own words on filming the last hour of daylight on 9/11: "it is what it is, it's just what happened" (paraphrase). **[extract]** https://www.thefader.com/2012/09/11/interview-william-basinski . The core story (2001, 9/11) is older than this file's window and is background only.
- *On Time Out of Time* (Temporary Residence, 8 March 2019) is built from source recordings from LIGO's interferometers of two black holes merging about 1.3 billion years ago. It was first commissioned for the 2017 installations *ER=EPR* and *Orbihedron* by Evelina Domnitch and Dmitry Gelfand, made with LIGO and Jean-Marc Chomaz for the *Limits of Knowing* exhibition in Berlin. **[extract]** https://temporaryresidence.com/products/trr311 (label page, not fetched; text via search extract) ; https://crackmagazine.net/2019/01/william-basinski-to-release-new-album-featuring-the-sounds-of-black-holes/
- *Lamentations* (Temporary Residence, 13 November 2020): twelve tracks built from tape loops and studies in his archive, some dating to 1979. **[extract]** https://xlr8r.com/?p=128213 ; https://www.synthtopia.com/?p=118635
- No 20th-anniversary *Disintegration Loops* release in 2021 or 2022 was found. **[extract, negative result]**
- **AI-free:** yes. Tape loops and recorded data. **Confidence:** High.

### 1.2 Grouper

## 3. Slowed + reverb, daycore and the label response

The signal chain is in [slushwave-sound.md](../../devices/slush-wave/docs/research/slushwave-sound.md) section 3.1. This section covers who did it and how it spread.

- **Origin:** Houston producer Jarylun "Slater" Moore, inspired by DJ Screw, began uploading slowed + reverb edits to YouTube; the first was Lil Uzi Vert's "20 Min" (January 2017). A re-upload passed 27 million views in three years. **[extract]** https://amp.knowyourmeme.com/memes/cultures/slowed-reverb (tertiary) ; https://clashmusic.com/features/how-slowed-reverb-became-the-soundtrack-to-gen-z-lockdown . No first-party Slater interview was found. The brief's suggested link to Lil Peep was not supported by any source. **Confidence:** Medium.
- **Lineage:** Chartmetric traces the slowed trend to Houston's chopped and screwed hip-hop, and there has been public pushback when TikTok users called chopped and screwed "slowed and reverb". **[extract]** https://hmc.chartmetric.com/nightcore-slowed-reverb-tiktok-remix/ ; https://jira.phpfox.com/p/chopped-and-screwed-vs-slowed-and-reverb (weak source)
- **Lockdown boom:** Clash frames slowed + reverb as "the soundtrack to Gen Z lockdown" (2020 to 2021); a student paper links its revival to "false nostalgia". **[extract]** https://clashmusic.com/features/how-slowed-reverb-became-the-soundtrack-to-gen-z-lockdown ; https://dbknews.com/2021/11/04/slow-reverb-remixes-nostalgia
- **Daycore and nightcore:** daycore is the slowed counterpart of nightcore (sped-up, pitched-up edits). One tool vendor says daycore sits at 80 to 90% speed with less reverb than slowed + reverb, but the terms overlap and that figure is not authoritative. **[extract]** https://hmc.chartmetric.com/nightcore-slowed-reverb-tiktok-remix/ ; https://rysupaudio.com/pages/nightcore-maker (vendor, weak)
- **Labels take over the format:** At The Disco released official sped-up and slowed versions of "House of Memories" after fan versions went viral on TikTok; Michael Bublé's sped-up "Sway" followed in May 2022. Spotify's "Sped Up Songs" playlist holds hours of officially re-released tracks. The UK Official Singles Chart groups official sped-up and slowed versions with the original song. **[extract]** https://www.musicradar.com/news/sped-up-songs-tiktok-mainstream ; https://www.hypebot.com/how-tiktok-remixes-are-reviving-music-from-the-2010s/ ; https://meme.com/memes/sped-up-tiktok-audios/md (weak). Critics argue official versions "strip creative power" from the community that started the trend. **[extract]** https://wp.dailybruin.com/2024/03/05/second-take-listener-creativity-remains-paramount-with-booming-popularity-of-nightcore-music
- **Research:** no peer-reviewed study of slowed + reverb itself was found. The nearest is Wilkie and Harrison (2025, *Music Perception*): longer reverberation time had a significant positive effect on "Sublimity" ratings including "Nostalgia", with little effect on "Unease" or "Vitality". It isolates reverb, not slowing. **[extract]** https://cms.mus.cam.ac.uk/publications/wilkie-reverberation/ . **Confidence:** Medium.
- **Background only:** "U Smile 800% Slower" (August 2010), posted to Reddit by Nick Pittsinger (Shamantis), used Paulstretch to stretch a 3:17 song past 35 minutes; NPR covered it two days later. Wikipedia credits a different name ("Varien"), which conflicts with NPR. **[extract]** https://www.npr.org/sections/therecord/2010/08/18/129283985/the-art-of-a-time-stretch ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Smile
- **AI-free:** yes. Varispeed plus reverb. **Takeaway [inference]:** the practice moved from a fan edit to an official product format in about five years, and the "artistic" version (slushwave) went further in slowness and modulation.

## 4. Extreme time-stretching: Paulstretch and its descendants

- **Paulstretch** (Paul's Extreme Sound Stretch) by Nasca Octavian Paul: "extreme" stretching, for example 3 minutes to 3 hours, and turning sounds into textures by spectral smoothing. It splits audio into windows processed separately; window size trades time resolution against frequency resolution; onset detection (added in 2.2, March 2011) disables stretching on windows with onsets so attacks stay quick. Stretch modes reach 10,000x, "HyperStretch" up to 10^18x. First release 0.0.1 on 27 May 2006; last listed release 2.2-2 on 6 March 2011. GPL v2. The page itself says it is not designed for mild stretches. **[read]** https://hypermammut.sourceforge.net/paulstretch/
- **How it works, from source:** PaulXStretch's `Source/PS_Source/Stretch.cpp` sets each FFT bin's phase to a random value (a Mersenne Twister draw scaled to 0 to 2π) and keeps its magnitude, at lines 205 to 252. Random phase per frame is what removes the "phasey" smearing of a classic phase vocoder and produces the smooth wash. **[read]** https://github.com/essej/paulxstretch (file `Source/PS_Source/Stretch.cpp`, last commit 2 October 2022)
- **PaulXStretch:** "based on the PaulStretch algorithm ... and specifically the PaulXStretch version from Xenakios"; "only suitable for radical transformation of sounds"; "Ambient music and sound design are probably the most suitable use cases. It can turn any short audio into an hours long ambient soundscape". Standalone on macOS, Windows, Linux and iOS, plug-in as AU and VST3 (AUv3 on iOS). GPL v3 with an App Store exception; copyright Xenakios 2017 to 2018 and Jesse Chappell 2022. **[read]** README at https://github.com/essej/paulxstretch ; first free release in 2018 covered by https://reaper.blog/2018/02/paulxstretch **[extract]**
- **Audacity:** Paulstretch is a built-in effect with two controls: Stretch Factor (default 10, minimum 1) and Time Resolution (analysis window, default 0.25 s, minimum about 0.001 s). Short windows keep rhythm but sound grainy; long windows (above about 2 s) give textures. It only slows down. **[extract]** https://manual.audacityteam.org/man/paulstretch.html ; https://doxy.audacityteam.org/_paulstretch_base_8cpp_source.html
- **Practice:** stretched versions of ambient classics are themselves a genre: Open Culture has twice featured a six-hour time-stretched *Music for Airports* (2019, re-posted 2025). **[extract]** https://openculture.com/2019/02/a-six-hour-time-stretched-version-of-brian-eno-music-for-airports.html
- **AI-free:** yes. FFT, random phase, overlap-add. **Confidence:** High.
- **Distinction worth keeping [inference]:** Paulstretch keeps pitch and kills time; varispeed (slowed + reverb, slushwave, Basinski's tape) drops pitch and time together. The slushwave doc asks which one slushwave's 5 to 10% extremes use; no source answers that.

## 5. Sustained tone, temperament and the organ

This is the most active "serious" strand of the period. Tuning *tools* are in [boundary-pushing-plugins.md](boundary-pushing-plugins.md) section 1; here the focus is the composers.

### 5.1 Kali Malone

- *The Sacrificial Code* (iDEAL Recordings, June 2019): close to two hours of pipe organ pieces, with additional organ pieces by Ellen Arkbro; Fact describes Malone as a Stockholm-based artist and organ tuner. Reissued by Ideologic Organ in April 2025. **[extract]** https://factmag.com/2019/06/28/kali-malone-the-sacrificial-code ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sacrificial_Code (tertiary)
- An earlier interview describes her working as an organ tuner in Sweden alongside Jan Börjeson, and explains that in equal temperament every pipe should beat equally. **[extract]** https://zweikommasieben.ch/articles/interviews/kali-malone-mind-wandering
- *Living Torch* (Portraits GRM, 7 July 2022): two movements, about 33 minutes, made at GRM in Paris in 2020 to 2021, commissioned for GRM's Acousmonium loudspeaker orchestra. Instruments: trombone (Mats Äleklint), bass clarinet (Isak Hedtjärn), an ARP 2500 modular synthesiser and a Boîte à Bourdons. Sources conflict on whether the ARP 2500 was Éliane Radigue's own instrument. **[extract]** https://hardwax.com/05146/kali-malone/living-torch ; https://ra.co/reviews/34982 ; https://archive.thevinylfactory.com/?p=195147
- *All Life Long* (Ideologic Organ, 9 February 2024): organ music played by Malone and Stephen O'Malley on historical meantone-tempered organs at Église Saint-François (Lausanne), Orgelpark (Amsterdam) and Malmö Konstmuseum; voice by Macadam Ensemble, brass by Anima Brass. The label copy says the historical meantone tunings and the "variable intonation of brass and voice" provide "points of emotional excavation within the harmony". The title piece appears twice: as an organ canon and, near the end, arranged for voice. Listings disagree on three or four organs. **[extract]** https://kalimalone.bandcamp.com/video ; https://acloserlisten.com/2024/02/06/kali-malone-all-life-long/ ; https://thequietus.com/?p=33797
- **AI-free:** yes. **Confidence:** High (works) / Medium (tuning specifics, since the TANK interview on tuning is paywalled).

### 5.2 Ellen Arkbro

- *For Organ and Brass* (Subtext, 2017) used an antique organ in meantone; *CHORDS* (Subtext, 2019) includes "CHORDS For Organ": nine interlocking organ chords across 16 minutes. The Quietus interview (24 July 2019) discusses intonation, organs and La Monte Young. **[extract]** https://forcedexposure.com/Catalog/SUB.029LP.html ; https://stage1.thequietus.com/?p=26832

### 5.3 Sarah Davachi

- Her work is described as built on extended durations, gradual changes in texture and overtones, psychoacoustics, tuning and intonation. *Cantus, Descant* (Late Music, September 2020); *Long Gradus* (2024, triple album exploring sustained tone, decay and resonance); *The Will of Tongues* (Late Music, 2026). A 2026 interview discusses meantone and Baroque organs with manual controls that change how air moves through the pipes. **[extract]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Davachi (tertiary) ; https://www.northrop.umn.edu/sarah-davachi ; https://www.treblezine.com/sarah-davachi-the-will-of-tongues-review/
- The brief's candidate title "The Head as Form'd in the Crier's Choir" was not found in any result. **[extract, negative result]**
- **AI-free:** yes. **Confidence:** Medium.

### 5.4 Kara-Lis Coverdale

- *Grafts* (Boomkat Editions, 26 April 2017; expanded reissue July 2020): a 22-minute piece in three parts for keys, samplers and EQs, described as exploring "digital-based ratio tuning". She later returned to the pipe organ (Berlin Jazz Festival and Issue Project Room, both 2018). **[extract]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grafts_(album) (tertiary) ; https://www.normanrecords.com/records/165009-kara-lis-coverdale-grafts ; https://issueprojectroom.org/video/kara-lis-coverdale-solo-organ
- **AI-free:** yes. **Confidence:** Medium.

**Takeaway [inference]:** in this strand the "effect" is acoustic beating between non-equal-tempered partials, heard over long durations. In Live 11 there is no native tuning system (Live 12 added one), so a device would have to synthesise its own tones or send MPE pitch bend.

## 7. Eno's generative work, 2016 to 2018

- *Reflection* (released 1 January 2017): a 54-minute album plus a generative iOS and Apple TV app designed by Peter Chilvers. Chilvers: "Moving the composition into software allowed an extra opportunity; the rules themselves could change with the time of day." The piece began as a long-form ambient work Eno had been refining; the app grew from a commission. **[extract]** https://kirkville.com/the-next-track-episode-35-musician-and-developer-peter-chilvers-on-brian-enos-album-and-app-reflection-2/ ; https://factmag.com/2016/12/15/brian-eno-reflection-generative-music-app-endless
- *Music for Installations* (UMC, 4 May 2018): six CDs (or nine LPs) of installation music from 1986 to 2017 and "future installations", half previously unreleased, with a new Eno essay. **[extract]** https://enoshop.co.uk/products/music-for-installations ; https://www.udiscovermusic.com/news/brian-eno-box-music-installations/
- *Bloom: 10 Worlds* (7 December 2018): a 10th-anniversary reworking of the 2008 *Bloom* app, with ten "worlds" of sounds, shapes, colours and "behavioural rules"; a generative player takes over when idle. First Android version. **[extract]** https://enoshop.co.uk/blogs/news/brian-eno-peter-chilvers-release-new-app-bloom-10-worlds ; https://factmag.com/2018/11/27/brian-eno-peter-chilvers-bloom-10-worlds
- **Background (incommensurable loops):** *Music for Airports* (1978) used tape loops of different lengths so that phrases realign differently on every pass; Eno described the studio method at the Imagination Conference, San Francisco, June 1996. The full transcript was not reached, and the word "incommensurable" did not appear in any source found. **[extract]** https://reverbmachine.com/blog/deconstructing-brian-eno-music-for-airports/ ; https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/brian-eno-music-for-airports-feature/
- **AI-free:** yes. Rule-based generative systems, not machine learning. **Confidence:** High.

## 8. The tape-loop revival

- **Amulets (Randall Taylor):** moved from dark electronic music towards ambient by adding guitar and cassette loops ("recording five seconds on a loop was a lot faster than recording an entire tape"); picked up tape loops via circuit bending in college; publishes cassette-loop tutorials and has run a course (building loops, four-track, multitracking). Pelagic Records: most sounds go through tape loops and pedals before reaching the DAW. A later project put a loop in a cassette shell designed to be "self-destructive, self-contained but reusable". 2025 album *Not Around but Through*. **[extract]** https://kgnu.org/?p=157962 ; https://lorezine.com/?p=10022 ; https://cdm.link/heres-make-tape-loop-cassette-tape/ ; https://wweek.com/music/2025/02/04/amulets-randall-taylor-finds-catharsis-on-his-newest-not-around-but-through
- **Hainbach (Stefan Paul Goetsch, Berlin):** YouTube channel since 2011 showing tape-loop music and repurposed test equipment. In "Three Tape Loops Destructing Over Three Hours" he wore piano loops down with knives and sandpaper; in another piece he fed viewers' hate comments onto loops that decayed over seven hours. **[extract]** https://disquiet.com/2019/02/01/sandpaper-is-a-form-of-change/ ; https://composer.spitfireaudio.com/en/articles/channeling-hainbach ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainbach_(musician) (tertiary)
- **AI-free:** yes. **Confidence:** Medium (practice well attested; little first-party technical detail reached).
- **Takeaway [inference]:** the YouTube tape scene makes *decay over a set duration* the piece. That is Basinski's accident turned into a deliberate, repeatable method, and it is easy to parameterise.

## 9. Granular and tape-emulating tools in everyday use

- **Make Noise Morphagene:** a stereo "microsound" tape module. Vari-Speed controls speed and direction with one control (retailers give +12 to -26 semitones); Gene-Size and Slide are the "Microsound Tools" that shrink and move the playback window. Sound On Sound reviewed it in July 2017 as a reworked Phonogene. **[extract]** https://imagescdn.juno.co.uk/manual/638949-01U.pdf (manual) ; https://www.soundonsound.com/node/4917005
- **Mutable Instruments Beads (2021):** a redesign of Clouds for live granular processing, with quality modes including a "cold digital" Clouds-like mode and a "scorched cassette" mode with wow and flutter. Mutable has since closed and its designs are open source **[background, unverified]**. **[extract]** https://en.clockfacemodular.com/products/mutable-instruments-beads-new ; https://modulargrid.net/e/offers/view/1407701
- **monome norns:** open-source sound computer running scripts; its sampling engine is softcut (Ezra Buchla and Brian Crabtree); scripts are shared on the lines forum. **[extract]** https://monome.org/docs/norns/
- **AI-free:** yes. **Confidence:** Medium.

## 10. Dark ambient

- **Cryo Chamber:** founded by Simon Heath (Atrium Carceri) after leaving Cold Meat Industry around 2011 to 2012; describes itself as a label "with a collaborative focus ... focusing on high quality dark ambient with a cinematic edge", now based in Oregon. ## 15. The streaming ambient boom and its critics

General coverage of Pelly and Chayka is in [boundary-pushing-music-philosophy.md](boundary-pushing-music-philosophy.md) section 10. Ambient-specific additions:

- **Perfect Fit Content:** Pelly's Harper's reporting (December 2024) says PFC was introduced to Spotify editors in 2017, sourcing tracks from production companies; coverage says about 20 songwriters stood behind more than 500 "ghost artists", mainly on jazz, classical, ambient and lo-fi playlists such as Deep Focus. Spotify called the accusations "categorically untrue". **[extract]** https://consequence.net/2024/12/spotify-perfect-fit-content-report ; https://musictech.com/news/music/spotify-uses-ghost-artists-on-playlists-report-claims/ ; https://cdm.link/liz-pelly-spotify-ghost-content/ . The book *Mood Machine* (Atria / One Signal, January 2025) also covers "the pseudoscientific therapeutics of 'chill'". **[extract]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mood_Machine (tertiary)
- **Spotify's own position:** functional music is "a very small amount" of total streams and "hasn't significantly changed in a decade"; most of it comes from established production music companies; background and production genres tend to command lower royalty rates; in 2024 Spotify began valuing noise recordings at a fraction of a music stream. **[extract]** https://support.spotify.com/au/artists/article/functional-music/
- **YouTube:** a 5 December 2025 Culture and Trends post describes "ambience channels" blending music and visuals for sleep, relaxation and focus. **[extract]** https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/ambient-music/
- **Scale claims:** Chartmetric valued the ambient market at US$1.5 billion in 2022 and reports growth in ambient artists' inclusion on Spotify editorial playlists since 2016. Spotify's statement above contradicts a simple "boom" story. **[extract]** https://hmc.chartmetric.com/ambient-music/ . **Confidence:** Medium.
- **AI angle:** Redef reports AI-driven companies producing short ambient soundscapes at scale, and functional-music company Endel claimed the sector generates 120 billion streams a year. These are *not* AI-free and are the contrast case for this file. **[extract]** https://redef.com/mix/music-mix-1677753767735 ; https://m.kuow.org/stories/why-the-ambient-music-market-is-booming
- **Artists' counter-statement:** Tim Hecker's *No Highs* (Kranky, 7 April 2023) was framed in its press text as set against "the deluge of false positive capitalist ambient currently in vogue"; a German announcement attributes to Hecker a challenge to conformist "wellness" positivity. **[extract]** https://headphonecommute.com/2023/07/01/tim-hecker-no-highs/ ; https://africanpaper.com/2023/02/23/neues-studioalbum-von-tim-hecker-auf-kranky/ . **Confidence:** Medium (the wellness wording comes through a secondary source).
- **Other related practice:** Loscil's *Clara* (Kranky, 28 May 2021) began as a three-minute piece recorded by a 22-piece Budapest string orchestra, cut to a 7-inch, scratched and "abused", then resampled into the album; Scott Morgan called the orchestra session "more of a business arrangement than a collaboration". **[extract]** https://nomoreworkhorse.com/2021/10/01/interview-with-scott-morgan-loscil-part-1/ ; https://200-percent.com/loscil-interview/ . A Winged Victory for the Sullen (Adam Wiltzie of Stars of the Lid, with Dustin O'Halloran) released *Invisible Cities* (Ninja Tune, 26 February 2021); Wiltzie told MusicRadar "If I can't fall asleep to a piece of music I've written it doesn't make the record". No 2024 release was confirmed. **[extract]** https://www.musicradar.com/news/a-winged-victory-for-the-sullen-if-i-cant-fall-asleep-to-a-piece-of-music-ive-written-it-doesnt-make-the-record

**Takeaway [inference]:** the line critics draw is not ambient versus non-ambient but authored process versus commissioned or automated filler. Wiltzie's sleep test and the PFC playlists aim at the same use; what differs is authorship and payment. A device cannot fix that, but it can favour material with a history (decayed, tuned, slowed from a specific source) over interchangeable pads.

## 16. Max for Live device ideas (Live 11 / Max 8)

All **[inference]** unless cited. Max objects named here are from general knowledge of Max 8 **[background, unverified]**; check each against the Max 8 reference before building. Nothing below needs Live 12 unless stated.

1. **Incommensurable Loops (audio effect).** Record a phrase into a buffer, then play it back through three to seven loop windows whose lengths are set by non-integer ratios (for example primes in milliseconds), each with its own fixed varispeed and pan, so the texture never repeats (section 7). Max: `buffer~`, `groove~` or `play~`, `gen~` for per-voice ramps. One "spread" knob from "in phase" to "never realigns".
2. **Disintegrator (audio effect).** A looper where every pass degrades the loop in place: write back through `peek~`/`poke~` or `gen~` with a per-pass high-frequency loss, small dropout probability, level loss and wow. A "lifetime" control sets how many passes until silence, so the piece has a set duration, as in Basinski, Hainbach's three-hour loops and Amulets' self-destructing cassette (sections 1, 8). Pair with a "freeze archive" button that stores the current state, since Basinski and Grouper reuse archives.
3. **Stretch Freeze (audio effect).** A Paulstretch-style spectral freeze: capture a buffer, read it with a pointer that moves at 1/10 to 1/1000 speed, and resynthesise each frame with random phases (the method in `Stretch.cpp`, section 4). Max: `pfft~` with `fftin~`, `cartopol~`, a `noise~`-driven phase, `poltocar~`, `fftout~`. Expose window size (Audacity's 0.25 s default is a sensible start) and a "smear" control. As the slushwave doc notes, a real-time effect cannot stretch its live input for ever, so it must work on a captured buffer.
4. **Temperament Drone (instrument).** A sustained-tone instrument with selectable historical temperaments (equal, quarter-comma meantone, Pythagorean, just) and a root, so thirds and fifths beat as in Malone, Arkbro and Davachi (section 5). Live 11 has no tuning system, so the device synthesises its own partials in `gen~` or drives MPE pitch bend. Show beat rates as a readout. Links to tuning work in [boundary-pushing-plugins.md](boundary-pushing-plugins.md) section 1.
5. **Time-of-Day Rules (MIDI effect).** Reflection's idea that "the rules themselves could change with the time of day" (section 7): use Max's `date` object to read the system clock and morph scale, density and register across the day. Can also run in an ASLSP-like "very slow" mode where one chord holds for hours (section 14).
6. **Markov and pattern machine (MIDI effect).** A Barbieri-style pattern sequencer (rotate, reverse, augment, skip) plus a first-order Markov mode trained on what the player plays. Max 8 includes `anal` (builds transition counts) and `prob` (plays weighted transitions), so this is AI-free and native. Live 11 clips also support note and velocity chance, which can feed it; Live 12's MIDI Generators would need an upgrade. **[extract]** for Live 11 chance: https://help.ableton.com/hc/en-us/articles/360019144299
7. **Data Score (MIDI effect).** Load a text or CSV file into `coll` or `text` and map columns to pitch, velocity and duration, following Basinski's LIGO piece (section 2). Ships with a small public-domain data set as a demo.
8. **Half-speed / octave-down layer (audio effect).** Already covered by the slush-wave device plans; do not duplicate. If added elsewhere, a simple "tape stop to half speed" macro over a buffer loop matches slowed + reverb practice (section 3).
